03What the research showed

User behaviour study

Individual research conducted by me.

When
Around October 2024
Participants
8 users across 2 hospitals
Who
Insurance heads, insurance desk staff (5), insurance supervisors
Where they work
The insurance desk is a high-pressure, fast-paced and sensitive environment. Staff talk to families of critically ill patients, and sometimes families whose patient has died. The chance of a mistake is very high.
Insights
Most hospitals have two teams at the insurance desk: a Pre-Auth team and a Claim team. The Pre-Auth team handles the pre-auth submissions before admission, answers insurer queries and updates the patient about the pre-auth approval status. The Claim team submits the final claim to the insurer post discharge for settlement, and answers settlement queries. The work moves between the two teams, and the handover happens at discharge.

06What I decided and why

01Two views, joined by a toggle

We landed on two views, each with its own primary action.

ViewForPrimary action
PA ViewPre-Auth teamNew Admission
Claim ViewClaim teamInitiate Settlement (claim submission)
Constraint

The platform serves both smaller and bigger hospitals. In smaller hospitals one person does both jobs, and in bigger hospitals the two teams need to collaborate. So instead of two separate views, we went with a toggle. Everyone switches between views in one click.

02Action-focused buckets

The old buckets showed status, not the next action. Staff kept clicking Approval Pending and searching for claims ready for Initiate Settlement.

The dashboard had to help staff decide faster, reduce cognitive load, and respond quickly. Both views were built around buckets that lead to an action.

Constraint

Each bucket has its own sub-levels. For example, drafts can be Pre-Auth, Enhancement, Query Reply or Discharge drafts. So each bucket drills down into secondary filters.

WhatWhy
A bucket for failed claimsBoth views have a Need Human Intervention (NHI) bucket for claims where automation failed and someone has to step in. Hospitals can now see failed claims in one place. Post launch, we expanded the NHI bucket with more sub-buckets.
Workflow speed
Colour codingGrey for drafts, orange for queries, blue for discharges and all processed, green for approved, amber for approval pending, red for denied, cancelled and NHI.
Lower cognitive load
Filtered downloadIn brainstorming sessions, we learned that teams download data to track their work. The claim submission team downloads at the end of the day to see what is pending and what they submitted. The download button exports all data or bucket-wise to Excel.
Workflow speed
A Claim Submission bucketClaim View has its own bucket for claims ready to submit, so staff no longer search for them one by one.
Workflow speed

03Scoped search instead of global search

GA showed Patient Name, Claim Number and UTR covered almost every search, so I recommended a scoped search across those three fields.

From GA data
Constraint

Hospitals asked for global search, but engineering found Elasticsearch too expensive and complex at our database size.

Technical constraint
The tradeoff

Hospitals didn't get the global search they asked for, but they got the searches they actually ran, with no new infrastructure.

Edge case handled

Search across views. With two views, a claim searched in the wrong view returned nothing. Now an empty search shows how many results exist in the other view, with a link to switch. It became one of the most used patterns after launch.

No results in either view. When a search finds nothing in both views, the table says so and names the search, and the line above it shows 0 results in the other view, so staff know the claim is not on the dashboard at all.

04Buckets agreed with the hospitals who use them

I worked out the buckets with the PM. Pre-Auth buckets follow time, and Claim buckets follow settlement status. We then ran four rounds of moderated feedback sessions with 5 hospitals.

The hardest call

One enterprise hospital pushed to replace the Discharge bucket with a filter, and the account manager was under pressure to agree. Four other hospitals in the same round confirmed the bucket worked, and the dashboard serves thousands of hospitals, so the bucket stayed. They signed anyway.

User feedback session

05Follow-up timing set per insurer

When a claim sat in Approval Pending, staff left IHX to email the insurer. Follow-up now sits in the claim list, next to how long the claim has been waiting.

The real decision was when to follow up. Each hospital has its own agreement with each insurer, so the waiting time is configurable per insurer.

Platform stickinessContract requirement

06What I left out: bulk settlement

Hotjar showed the Claim team settling claims one by one every evening. We designed a bulk workflow, but shipping it alongside a structural redesign would have added risk. We shipped the structure first and planned bulk settlement for v2.

    How I know the redesign drove the 51%

    Only contracted hospitals got the new dashboard, while non-contracted hospitals stayed on the old UI. Over the same period, time dropped on the new dashboard and stayed flat on the old one, so it wasn't seasonality or a platform-wide change.

    09What I learned

    Look past the feature request. Hospitals asked for global search. What they needed was a dashboard that matched their two workflows, and the study, Hotjar and GA showed that before I designed anything.

    What I would have done if I had more time. I would have tried out more design variations for where the secondary filters sit on the claim listing screen.

    What this shows about how I work

    I design around how teams actually work, not around the feature they ask for. I let research and data decide what to build and what to leave out. I collaborate and work with all stakeholders from the beginning to the end to understand each stakeholder's perspective, test the change with the people who use it, and measure the result.
