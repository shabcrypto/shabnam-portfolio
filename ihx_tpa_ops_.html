Case study · IHX claims dashboard

Two teams, one dashboard.

How restructuring one screen cut claim submission time by 51%

Role
Product designer (Research, Interaction design, Prototyping)
Timeline
4 weeks, March to April 2025
Users
Hospital insurance desks
Read the detailed case study

01 Context

IHX helps hospitals manage every insurance claim in one place.

IHX is a health-tech platform for hospitals to manage the full claim lifecycle in one place, instead of logging into 35+ insurer portals. It processes 2,00,000+ claims a month for 18,000+ providers, about 40% of India's claim volume.

Pain point

03 User behaviour study

The insurance desk is high-pressure, fast-paced and sensitive.

Staff talk to families of critically ill patients, and sometimes families whose patient has died. The chance of a mistake is very high.

What I didI ran this study on my own. I talked to the insurance desk staff and watched them work, 8 users across 2 hospitals.

Insight

Most hospitals have two teams at the insurance desk: a Pre-Auth team and a Claim team. The work moves between the two teams, and the handover happens at discharge.

05 The insight

It wasn’t a screen problem.

Most hospitals run two teams at the insurance desk: a Pre-Auth team and a Claim team. Big enterprise and multi-speciality hospitals have both teams, while in small and mid-size hospitals one person does both jobs. The dashboard had to work for both, but it treated everyone as one user.

The buckets showed status, not the next action. So staff searched claim by claim to find what to do next.

Who I designed for

Suresh · Insurance Supervisor

06 Information architecture

Each view follows one pattern: stage buckets, then secondary filters, then the table.

What I didAlong with my product manager, I revamped the information architecture and worked out the buckets for both views.

07 Pain point to design solution

What the research asked for, and what I designed

What I didI let research and data decide what to build and what to leave out.

  1. Pain point

    The dashboard treated two hospital teams as one user

    Design solution

    PA View and Claim View, joined by a toggle

  2. Pain point

    Staff searched for claims one by one

    Design solution

    Buckets that lead to an action, then secondary filters, then one table

  3. Pain point

    Staff kept searching for claims ready for Initiate Settlement

    Design solution

    A Claim Submission bucket for claims ready to submit

  4. Pain point

    Missed discharge queries

    Design solution

    A Query Reply Pending bucket, next to Discharges in PA View

  5. Pain point

    Staff left IHX to email the insurer

    Design solution

    Follow-up in the claim list, next to how long the claim has been waiting

  6. Pain point

    Hospitals asked for global search

    Design solution

    Scoped search on Patient Name, Claim Number and UTR, the fields behind 80%+ of searches

08 High-fidelity design and prototype

What I didI was the only designer on the team. I designed the UI, built hi-fi prototypes, ran 4 rounds of moderated feedback sessions with 5 hospitals, presented the design rationale to senior stakeholders for approval, and handed off to the developers.

Pain points

    09 Trade-offs and constraints

    What I chose not to build, and why.

    What I didI recommended scoped search from the GA data, and kept the Discharge bucket when one enterprise hospital pushed to replace it, because four other hospitals confirmed it worked.

    Constraint

    One dashboard had to serve small hospitals, where one person does both jobs, and big ones with two separate teams.

    Trade-off

    Hospitals asked for global search, but it was too expensive and complex at our database size. Scoped search covers the searches they actually ran.

    Deferred

    Bulk settlement was designed, then moved to v2 to lower launch risk.

    Constraint

    Each hospital has its own agreement with each insurer, so follow-up timing is set per insurer.
