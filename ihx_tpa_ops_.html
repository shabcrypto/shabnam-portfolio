The business problem

01IHX

IHX is a health-tech platform for hospital insurance operations in India. Hospitals manage the full claim lifecycle in one place, instead of logging into 35+ insurer portals. It processes 2,00,000+ claims a month for 18,000+ providers, about 40% of India's claim volume.

Of the 35+ insurers, 7 connect through an API. The other 28 run on RPA and email extraction. When that automation fails, the claim disappears and the hospital can't see it. The design had to account for that.

02Why we redesigned it

The Ops Dashboard is where hospital insurance staff spend their working day. It was inherited from IHX's parent insurance company. When IHX became its own company, nobody went back to check how hospital teams actually work.

Hospitals were complaining, and the complaints reached us through account managers and sales. At the same time, a new contract required a follow-up feature, and the dashboard had no sensible place for it.

So the dashboard had to be redesigned. The work started with the research and the data, to understand what was going wrong for hospitals.

What the research showed

Over a year and a half on IHX products, I had run a user behaviour study with hospital insurance staff: 15+ users across 5+ hospitals and 6+ teams. Account managers and hospital staff added more input during the project.

01It's two workflows, not one

Pre-Auth team

Time-critical · patient-facing

They start the claim before admission, answer insurer queries and start the discharge. A delayed discharge costs the hospital a bed.

Claim team

Finance-critical

They submit the final claim after discharge, settle claims at the end of the day and reconcile payments.

Supervisors

Oversight, not action

They check their team's workload rather than act, so they need counts across several statuses at once.

What I decided and why

01Two views, joined by a toggle

I took the study findings to the PM, who checked them against what he was hearing from hospitals. We landed on two views: a PA View for the Pre-Auth team and a Claim View for the Claim team.

The obvious next step was a fixed view per role. I recommended a toggle instead, because in smaller hospitals one person does both jobs. Split teams each get their own view, and everyone else switches in one click.

Each view follows one pattern: stage buckets, then secondary filters, then the table. Five later IHX modules reused it with no new architecture work.

02Scoped search instead of global search

Hospitals had asked for global search since launch, but engineering found Elasticsearch too expensive and complex at our database size. GA showed Patient Name, Claim Number and UTR covered almost every search, so I recommended a scoped search across those three fields, with partial match.

The tradeoff

Hospitals didn't get the global search they asked for, but they got the searches they actually ran, with no new infrastructure.

03Buckets agreed with the hospitals who use them

I worked out the buckets with the PM. Pre-Auth buckets follow time (Awaiting Discharge splits today and tomorrow), and Claim buckets follow settlement status. We then ran four rounds of moderated feedback sessions with 2 to 3 hospitals each, covering Pre-Auth staff, Claim staff and supervisors.

  1. Round 1

    What we learned. Both teams found their own view without help. Pre-Auth staff didn't need a Discharge Tomorrow bucket.

    What changed. The PA/Claim split was confirmed. That bucket was removed.

  2. Round 2

    What we learned. Some bucket names weren't clear. Staff asked for a date filter. Supervisors needed several statuses at once.

    What changed. Names reworked.

  3. Round 3

    What we learned. Bucket names agreed across five hospitals.

    What changed. Final names. Multi-select filters and the date filter added.

  4. Round 4

    What we learned. Scoped search and the follow-up feature worked.

    What changed. Follow-up timing made configurable per insurer.

The hardest call

One enterprise hospital pushed hard to replace the Discharge bucket with a filter, and the account manager was under pressure to agree. But the dashboard serves thousands of hospitals, and four others in the same round confirmed the bucket worked. The bucket stayed. The reasoning went back through the account manager, and their feedback was logged. They signed anyway.

04Follow-up timing set per insurer

When a claim sat in Approval Pending with no reply, staff left IHX to email the insurer from their own inbox. The new structure gave follow-up a natural home: an action in the claim list, next to how long the claim has been waiting.

The real decision was when to follow up. Each insurer works on its own timeline, and each hospital has its own agreement with each insurer, so a single default would have been wrong for most. The waiting time is configurable per insurer, in Manage Configuration, and shows in the claim list as an elapsed time column.

05What I left out: bulk settlement

Hotjar showed the Claim team settling claims one by one every evening. We designed a bulk workflow, but shipping it alongside a structural redesign would have added risk on two fronts. We shipped the structure first and planned bulk settlement for v2.

Design details that changed the outcome

  • A Claim Submission bucket. Claim submission is when the pre-auth becomes a final claim at discharge. Claim View now has its own bucket for claims ready to submit, with eligible claims at the top, so staff no longer search for them one by one.
  • Search across views. With two views, a claim searched in the wrong view returned nothing. Now an empty search shows how many results exist in the other view, with a link to switch. It's one line of copy, and it became one of the most used patterns after launch.
  • Multi-select filters. Single-select chips became a checkbox dropdown, so supervisors can see several statuses at once.
  • Date filters. A patient name search returned every claim on record for that name. A date filter narrows it to the relevant ones.
  • One bucket card, everywhere. Label, count, urgency and filter chips, with consistent colours: indigo for approvals, amber for queries, teal for discharge, red for denied. The same card runs across seven IHX surfaces.

    How I know the redesign drove the 51%. Only contracted hospitals got the new dashboard, while non-contracted hospitals stayed on the old UI. Over the same period, time dropped on the new dashboard and stayed flat on the old one, so it wasn't seasonality or a platform-wide change.

    Since launch, we've kept adding buckets and filters based on hospital feedback.

    What I learned

    • Look past the feature request. Hospitals asked for global search. What they needed was a dashboard that matched their two workflows, and the study, Hotjar and GA showed that before I designed anything.
    • Let data settle the arguments. Scoped search came from GA, not opinion. The Discharge bucket was settled by four hospitals in the same round, not by whoever pushed hardest.
    • Ship one big change at a time. Bulk settlement was ready, and we held it for v2 so the new structure could land on its own.

    If there was more time

    1. Measure the Pre-Auth side too. I only measured the Claim team's time. Pre-Auth is the time-critical team, so I'd measure how long they take to find and answer an insurer query.
    2. Bring supervisors in earlier. Their need to see several statuses at once only surfaced in round 2, which meant reworking the filters. I'd observe supervisors in the user study itself.

    What this shows about how I work: I design complex B2B workflows around how people actually work, and I use data to decide what to build and what to leave out.
